Core questionWhich stores and product categories are driving year-on-year sales growth, and where is the business slipping?

Business Context & Problem

Designed a single dashboard comparing sales performance across five London stores and five product categories against the previous year, so the business can see where growth is coming from and where it is slipping.

Technical Execution & Tools Used

  • Power Query — cleaning and shaping the raw sales data ready for modelling.
  • DAX — year-on-year growth measures, average sales per store and per transaction, and KPI cards with monthly trend lines.
  • Power BI — interactive year, month and store slicers, a bubble chart with average reference lines, a dual-axis combo chart, and a colour-scaled category share matrix.

Key Business Insights & Impact

  • Overall growth: total sales reached £143.43K (+7.7%), driven by more transactions (596, +9.8%) rather than bigger baskets, as average spend per transaction dipped 1.8% to £240.65.
  • Store performance: every store grew, led by City of London (+23.6%) and Carnaby Street (+17.8%). Regent Street handles by far the most transactions but grew the least (+3.4%) at below-average spend.
  • Category mix: Home accounts for around 50% of sales in every store. Accessories (+31.8%) and Home (+21.5%) grew strongly, while Womenswear (−33.2%), Footwear (−13.6%) and Menswear (−11.8%) declined.

Recommendations

  1. Recover Womenswear volume. It fell 33.2% but has the highest average transaction value (£256), so it is worth recovering rather than dropping.
  2. Raise basket size at Regent Street. It handles by far the most transactions but grew the least (+3.4%) at below-average spend, a good place to cross-sell Home and Accessories.
  3. Protect Home as the core category. Home makes up around half of sales in every store and grew 21.5%.
  4. Build on Accessories growth. Accessories grew fastest (+31.8%) and pairs naturally with Home for cross-selling.
  5. Learn from City of London and Carnaby Street. The two fastest-growing stores (+23.6% and +17.8%) are the benchmark for what is working.
  6. Investigate Footwear and Menswear. Both declined (−13.6% and −11.8%) and need a closer look before the trend sets in.
  7. Lift average spend, not just footfall. Growth came from more transactions (+9.8%) while average spend per transaction dipped 1.8% to £240.65.
